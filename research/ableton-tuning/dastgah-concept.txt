Persian Dastgah Concept: Tuning and Musical Information
Source: https://tuning.ableton.com/persian-radif/dastgah-concept/

Core Concept of Dastgah
  The term "Dastgah" originates from hand position terminology on instruments,
  literally meaning "the position (gah) of the hand (dast)." Each Dastgah represents
  a unique tonal space containing melodic patterns called Gusheh organized into twelve
  main modal groups. Together, these form the Radif, a repertoire of over 250 melodic
  patterns central to Iranian classical music.

Farhat's 17-Tone Scale Framework
  Hormoz Farhat developed an unequal 17-tone scale accounting for fixed-fret
  instruments like tar and setar. His theory emphasizes:
    Flexible intervals rather than rigid pitch values
    Two neutral tone categories: 125-145 cents and 150-170 cents
    Avoidance of intervals smaller than 90 cents in modal structures
    No chromatic movement in classical Iranian melodies

Historical Context
  Farhat rejected Western comparative approaches, asserting no "scale" concept
  exists in Iranian music. His framework represents pitches as a compositional
  palette generating modes typically containing 4-5 pitches, occasionally reaching 7.
